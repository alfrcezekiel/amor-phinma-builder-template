# Iloilo Tourism Platform — Session 1 Pitch

## The script

**The person**

Last weekend I watched my friend John try to plan a trip around Iloilo. He wanted to find mountains to hike, good food spots, and maybe a heritage site to visit. He sat with his laptop, searching Google for each thing separately, scrolling through Facebook pages, then asking friends in a group chat. After two hours he picked a familiar mountain and a restaurant he already knew. Later he found out about a great local festival he missed entirely. Then he spent another hour searching for hotels, comparing prices across different websites. This happens every weekend.

**Today**

Right now John's workaround is a mess. He jumps between Google, Facebook, and random websites for every type of destination he wants to visit. Mountains here, food spots there, heritage sites somewhere else. Then he starts over for accommodations, checking hotel sites, Facebook pages, and booking platforms. Information is everywhere and nowhere. He misses great spots and good deals because there's no single place to see what's available. It costs him 2-3 hours every time he plans a trip.

**Our fix**

We're building a centralized web platform for all types of destinations in Iloilo — mountains, food spots, walking areas, festivals, nature parks, and heritage sites — plus hotels and condominiums. Iloilo is special — it's the Heart of the Philippines, known for its Spanish colonial churches, the Dinagyang Festival, and genuine hospitality. One website where John can see everything in one place — name, location, photo, and a short description. He opens the site, clicks on a category card like mountains to filter the list, browses the destinations, clicks a spot to see details, then browses accommodations to find where to stay. No more scattered searches.

**The demo moment**

In Session 3 we'll show you a live website with destinations across all six categories — mountains, food spots, walking areas, festivals, nature parks, and heritage sites — plus hotels and condominiums from Iloilo. You'll see the category cards, click one to filter the list, view destination details, and browse accommodations. It will work in the browser, no login required.

**How we'll know**

We'll have Elizabeth and Mark test the app before the final demo. They'll tell us if it actually helps them discover spots and accommodations they didn't know about. If they find it useful, we've succeeded.

## Hard questions

**Q1: Is this a real problem or just hypothetical?**

We based this on watching John struggle, but we admit our observation wasn't as detailed as it should have been. We're going to watch him plan another trip this weekend and take notes on exactly what he searches, how long it takes, and what he misses. We'll bring that data to Session 2.

**Q2: Isn't six categories too much for an MVP?**

We started with just mountains and accommodations, but after watching John we realized he actually needs to browse multiple types of destinations in one trip. The categories are all displayed in a simple grid with cards, and the data is the same structure across all of them. Adding more categories doesn't add complexity — it's just more data. We still have one type of user and one core job: discover destinations and find a place to stay.

**Q3: Why can't a Google Sheet or Facebook page solve this?**

A Google Sheet would be better than nothing, but it doesn't feel like a real platform and it's hard to share. A Facebook page still mixes everything together with posts and ads. A dedicated website gives us control over the experience and room to grow features later. But if the tourism office published a well-organized site, yes, that would solve this problem without us building anything.
